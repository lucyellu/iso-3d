Roughly 117 billion people have been born since our species appeared, and about 109 billion have died. Each dot is 20 million of them, sorted by the belief they most likely held when they died. How the numbers are built

  • Births per era follow the Population Reference Bureau's estimate of about 117 billion people ever born, starting 190,000 years ago (PRB, 2022), extended to 2026.
  • Within each era, births track estimated population, and deaths are births minus population growth.
  • The world is split into seven regions, each with its own population share and belief mix over time. The world view adds them up.
  • Modern shares follow Pew Research Center's 2020 global religious composition. The 1900 and 1970 shares lean on the World Christian Database.
  • About 45% of all deaths ever were children under 15. That share held steady until the 1800s and has since fallen below 10%.

Judgment calls

  • Uncertainty ranges assume every error within an era pushes the same way: ±50% before 40,000 BCE, ±40% to 3000 BCE, ±30% to 1500, ±20% to 1800, ±10% to 1950, and ±4% after. They are wide on purpose.
  • Syncretism is ignored. Someone in Tang China who honored ancestors and visited Buddhist temples counts in one realm.
  • Everyone who died before about 100,000 BCE goes to The Unrecorded, fading to zero by 40,000 BCE.
  • In the single-tradition modes, anything the tradition leaves to divine judgment is either set by the slider or left out. Cause-of-death shares for the Norse and Aztec rules are rough estimates.

What the model leaves out

  • Individual deeds. No mode judges anyone's life, only which group they belonged to or how they died.
  • Conversion during life. People count in the tradition they most likely held at death.
  • Hundreds of distinct Indigenous and folk religions share one realm for readability, which flattens real differences.
  • Sources: PRB, Pew Research.
